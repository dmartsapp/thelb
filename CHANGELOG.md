# Changelog

Notable changes to theLB, newest first. Versions follow [semantic versioning](https://semver.org); a change that scripts could notice is called out in bold.

## v0.1.0 - unreleased

The first code: a director on its own node; directors forming a cluster with Raft - joining, approval, the leader holding the control VIP, failover; and backends, pools, services and IPVS direct routing, with health checks from both sides.

- **The command line**: `thelb director`, `backend`, `status`, `doctor`, `generate` and `admin` (`approve`, `refuse`, `remove`, `pool add|remove`, `service add|remove`, `assign`, `unassign`, `secret show`), and `--version`. Every command runs only as root (effective user ID 0); the help and `--version` run for anyone. Exit statuses: `0` done, `1` failed, `2` used wrongly. What this build does not do yet says so, and exits `1`.
- **`thelb director`** looks for the cluster on the control VIP - a TLS handshake on `VIP:port`, and an ARP probe or neighbour solicitation for the VIP - and joins it; with `--bootstrap`, when nothing answers for 5 seconds, it creates it: the secret files if missing, the cluster, its certificate authority and the director's own certificate, all in memory. It tells systemd it is ready (`Type=notify`) and alive (`WatchdogSec=`), and renews its certificate. On `SIGTERM` a leader hands leadership over first; then it stops and leaves nothing behind. A control VIP that a crash left on the interface is removed at the next start; one theLB did not add is refused.
- **The cluster**: Raft among the directors ([hashicorp/raft](https://github.com/hashicorp/raft) v1.8.0, in memory only: every start is a new member identity), over mutual TLS 1.3 on the control port, with the cluster's state - its CA, approved and pending nodes, certificates, revocations, the leader - replicated to every director. **The leader holds the control VIP**: it adds it (`/32` or `/128`, labelled `eth0:thelb`, address protocol 116), announces it (gratuitous ARP, or unsolicited neighbour advertisements, three times over two seconds), and gives it up the moment it stops leading - a leader cut off from the others gives it up by itself. Directors check in with the leader every second.
- **Joining**: both sides prove they know the directors' secret, bound to the TLS connection; a director with another secret stops, as out of date. **Every new director waits for approval** - `thelb admin approve <name>`, or `--match '<regex>'` with a confirmation or `--yes` - from any director; `refuse` stops it, `remove` revokes its certificate and takes it out of Raft. An approved director that restarts, with the same node fingerprint, address and MAC address, rejoins by itself; after a full stop, every director is approved again.
- **The secret files**: at the first bootstrap, a missing `--secret-file` or `--backend-secret-file` is created with a new 256-bit secret - exclusively, mode `0600`, never overwritten: the one thing theLB writes. A secret is read only from a regular file that root owns and no one else can read, and is never logged: its type prints as `[redacted]` everywhere.
- **`thelb generate secret`** prints a new secret; **`thelb admin secret show --role director|backend`** prints the running director's.
- **The node fingerprint**: a hash of the machine's application-specific ID (from `/etc/machine-id`, which never leaves the machine), its SMBIOS UUID where it has one, and the MAC address of the interface the node joins through.
- **The certificate authority**: an ECDSA P-256 CA in memory, and director certificates for `spiffe://dmarts.app/thelb/<cluster>/director/<name>` with the node's addresses, valid for 24 hours and renewed at two thirds.
- **`thelb status`**: **a summary by default** - the cluster, and how many directors, backends, services and VIPs are up - with views for each part: `directors`, `backends`, `vips`, and `all`. Together: the cluster with its leader and term, every director with its state and last check-in, the certificates, the pending nodes, and the control VIP and who holds it - as tables in the style of a SQL client's results, or as JSON (`--json`); one table by name; `--watch`. On a director that does not lead, the check-ins come from the leader. Pools, offers, service VIPs, services, and every backend serving a service with its two checks, its weight and IPVS's counters. On a backend, `status` shows that backend alone: its role, the cluster, its offers and the service each is assigned to - not the VIP - and its node fingerprint.
- **The local socket** `/run/thelb/thelb.sock`: root only, by its mode and by the kernel's answer for who is calling; one theLB per node; a socket left by a crash is replaced.
- **Logs**: one line per event - `<time> [<module>] OK|WARN|ERROR <what happened> key=value ...` - on standard error.
- **Building and checking**: `make build`, `make check` (format, vet for this platform and Linux, race-enabled tests, lint, `govulncheck`), `make e2e` - the kernel tests as root, then the built binary in a lab of network namespaces: a bootstrap, joins and approvals, a crashed leader, a planned stop, a partition, removal, refusal, a wrong secret, a full stop, and three backends serving a service through IPVS - traffic spread, a failing application drained and back, the leader crashing with the services, a backend stopping, one removed - with nothing sent on the machine's own network; `make lab` builds the same lab to try by hand, `test/lab.sh` drives it - and `make remote-check` and `make remote-e2e` for a Linux machine over ssh.
- **`thelb backend`**: enrols on the control VIP with the backends' secret and waits for a director's approval (`admin approve`, as directors do); offers what it serves (`--offer tcp/8080,check=http:/healthz`, any number); sends its heartbeat every 2 seconds; holds the VIPs of the services its offers are assigned to on its own dummy interface, `thelb0`, with `arp_ignore` 1 and `arp_announce` 2; checks its applications; renews its certificate. On `SIGTERM` it tells the leader first, which drains it, then removes `thelb0` and restores its parameters. Removed by a director, it stops.
- **Pools, services and assignments**: `admin pool add <name> --vip <first/prefix> --vippool-count <n>` (inside its network, no network or broadcast address, no overlap with another pool, the control VIP or a node's address, on an interface the leader has there); `admin service add <name> --pool <pool> --proto tcp|udp|sctp --port <port>` takes the pool's lowest free address, probed on the network first; `admin assign <service> <node>:<proto>/<port> ... [--weight node=n]` checks that the offer is the backend's, its protocol and port are the service's, and that the leader reaches the backend directly. In this build an application's port must be its service's: a backend that maps ports comes later.
- **The data plane**: the leader adds the service VIPs to their pools' interfaces and announces them, programs IPVS over netlink - no `ipvsadm` - with direct routing and `wrr` by default, pins each backend's address to its MAC address, and sets `sloppy_tcp`, `expire_nodest_conn` and `expire_quiescent_template`, restoring them when it stops leading. A backend gets its weight only while both its own check and the leader's pass, its heartbeat is current and it holds the VIP; otherwise weight 0. A director that crashed removes the VIPs, IPVS services and pins it left when it starts again.
- **Dependencies**: hashicorp/raft and what it brings (go-hclog, go-metrics, go-msgpack, golang-lru, go-immutable-radix, and their own); everything else is Go's standard library.
