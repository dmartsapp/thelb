# Changelog

Notable changes to theLB, newest first. Versions follow [semantic versioning](https://semver.org); a change that scripts could notice is called out in bold.

## v0.1.0 - unreleased

The first code: a director on its own node; directors forming a cluster with Raft - joining, approval, the leader holding the control VIP, failover; and backends, pools, services and IPVS direct routing, with health checks from both sides.

- **`thelb status` tables no longer end with a row count** (`(3 rows)`): a blank line still separates them.
- **A planned stop takes a director out of the vote, so two directors survive either one's planned stop**: a director stopped by `SIGTERM`, `Ctrl+C` or `SIGHUP` - after handing leadership over, if it led - has the leader take its member identity out of Raft (API op `leave`). Raft is in memory, so that identity never came back, and it counted towards the majority while never voting again: with two directors, the one left could neither keep its leadership nor win an election. It stays approved and rejoins without approval; **`thelb status` shows it as `stopped`**, not `down`. A crash still counts: use three directors to survive one.
- **Connections in progress survive a failover**: every director runs IPVS's connection-synchronisation daemon as backup, and the leader one as master too, on the control VIP's interface - multicast to `224.0.0.81`, UDP `8848`, with a sync ID from the cluster's ID - so the director that takes over knows each connection's backend. The roles follow leadership; a master a crash left is removed at the next start, and a daemon someone stopped is started again within 10 s. **Directors now need multicast between them, and a trusted network: the kernel's sync messages are not authenticated.**
- **Fixed: an IPVS service removed from the cluster while a director was down** stayed in that director's kernel, still sending to its backends, after the director's crash; a director now removes, at its start, any IPVS service on a pool's address, besides those the cluster has.
- **A control VIP that answers on the network while its port drops connections** - a firewall on the leader, most often - is now reported as that (`<MAC> holds <VIP>, but nothing accepts a connection on port <port>: open the control port ...`), not as a VIP another program has taken.
- **`SIGHUP` stops theLB cleanly**, as `SIGTERM` does: a director or backend run in a terminal or over SSH no longer dies at once when the session closes, leaving its VIPs on the interface, answered for, with nothing behind them.
- **The command line**: `thelb director`, `backend`, `status`, `doctor`, `generate` and `admin` (`approve`, `refuse`, `remove`, `pool add|remove`, `service add|remove`, `assign`, `unassign`, `secret show`), and `--version`. Every command runs only as root (effective user ID 0); the help and `--version` run for anyone. Exit statuses: `0` done, `1` failed, `2` used wrongly. What this build does not do yet says so, and exits `1`.
- **`thelb director`** looks for the cluster on the control VIP - a TLS handshake on `VIP:port`, and an ARP probe or neighbour solicitation for the VIP - and joins it; with `--bootstrap`, when nothing answers for 5 seconds, it creates it: the secret files if missing, the cluster, its certificate authority and the director's own certificate, all in memory. It tells systemd it is ready (`Type=notify`) and alive (`WatchdogSec=`), and renews its certificate. On `SIGTERM` a leader hands leadership over first; then it stops and leaves nothing behind. A control VIP that a crash left on the interface is removed at the next start; one theLB did not add is refused.
- **The cluster**: Raft among the directors ([hashicorp/raft](https://github.com/hashicorp/raft) v1.8.0, in memory only: every start is a new member identity), over mutual TLS 1.3 on the control port, with the cluster's state - its CA, approved and pending nodes, certificates, revocations, the leader - replicated to every director. **The leader holds the control VIP**: it adds it (`/32` or `/128`, labelled `eth0:thelb`, address protocol 116), announces it (gratuitous ARP, or unsolicited neighbour advertisements, three times over two seconds), and gives it up the moment it stops leading - a leader cut off from the others gives it up by itself. Directors check in with the leader every second.
- **Joining**: both sides prove they know the directors' secret, bound to the TLS connection; a director with another secret stops, as out of date. **Every new director waits for approval** - `thelb admin approve <name>`, or `--match '<regex>'` with a confirmation or `--yes` - from any director; `refuse` stops it, `remove` revokes its certificate and takes it out of Raft. An approved director that restarts, with the same node fingerprint, address and MAC address, rejoins by itself; after a full stop, every director is approved again.
- **The secret files**: at the first bootstrap, a missing `--secret-file` or `--backend-secret-file` is created with a new 256-bit secret - exclusively, mode `0600`, never overwritten: the one thing theLB writes. A secret is read only from a regular file that root owns and no one else can read, and is never logged: its type prints as `[redacted]` everywhere.
- **Drift repaired, and systemd's watchdog told only while theLB works**: the leader applies only what its plan changed - nothing while nothing changes - and every 10 s compares the kernel whole: IPVS services, destinations and weights, the service VIPs and the control VIP on their interfaces, the pinned neighbour entries, the IPVS parameters; a backend compares its `thelb0` and VIPs, its nftables table, `thelbt0` and its parameters. What someone removed or changed is put back, and logged as `WARN drift repaired: ...`. `WATCHDOG=1` goes to systemd only while the loops go round - a director's check-ins and, while it leads, its data plane; a backend's heartbeat and kernel repair - and a loop 30 s overdue is logged and stops it, so systemd restarts a hung theLB.
- **Offers by name, made while a backend runs**: `thelb offer add <name> <proto>/<port>[,check=...]` and `thelb offer remove <name>`, on a backend; `--offer [<name>=]<proto>/<port>` at start, optional. The leader runs the offer's check against the application before it records the offer, and refuses it, with the reason, while it does not answer; a backend offers an `--offer` once its own check of it passes, and one refused again when its check passes again, or 10 s later. The cluster keeps a backend's offers through its restarts, and the backend keeps them through the directors' outage, offering them again to a cluster created again once approved. `thelb admin assign <service> <node>:<name>` (or `<node>:<proto>/<port>`); `thelb status offers` lists every offer, its backend's own check, and its service. The heartbeat no longer carries the offers.
- **A service's VIP comes up last**: the leader holds a service's VIP only while a backend serves one of its services, and adds it only after IPVS has that backend; the VIP goes first when the last one stops serving. The data plane now releases VIPs, pins backends, programs IPVS, and takes VIPs, in that order.
- **`make e2e` builds its own binary**, `bin/e2e/thelb`, never the `bin/thelb` someone may be running.
- **`thelb generate commands`**, on a director, prints the `thelb admin` commands that create the cluster's pools, services and assignments again - each service on its VIP - with the nodes to approve as a comment: a script to keep, for after every director has stopped. theLB never reads it.
- **`thelb generate secret`** prints a new secret; **`thelb admin secret show --role director|backend`** prints the running director's.
- **The node fingerprint**: a hash of the machine's application-specific ID (from `/etc/machine-id`, which never leaves the machine), its SMBIOS UUID where it has one, and the MAC address of the interface the node joins through.
- **The certificate authority**: an ECDSA P-256 CA in memory, and director certificates for `spiffe://dmarts.app/thelb/<cluster>/director/<name>` with the node's addresses, valid for 24 hours and renewed at two thirds.
- **`thelb status`**: **a summary by default** - the cluster, and how many directors, backends, services and VIPs are up - with views for each part: `directors`, `backends`, `vips`, and `all`. Together: the cluster with its leader and term, every director with its state and last check-in, the certificates, the pending nodes, and the control VIP and who holds it - as tables in the style of a SQL client's results, or as JSON (`--json`); one table by name; `--watch`. On a director that does not lead, the check-ins come from the leader. Pools, offers, service VIPs, services, and every backend serving a service with its two checks, its weight and IPVS's counters. On a backend, `status` shows that backend alone: its role, the cluster, its offers and the service each is assigned to - not the VIP - and its node fingerprint.
- **The local socket** `/run/thelb/thelb.sock`: root only, by its mode and by the kernel's answer for who is calling; one theLB per node; a socket left by a crash is replaced.
- **Logs**: one line per event - `<time> [<module>] OK|WARN|ERROR <what happened> key=value ...` - on standard error.
- **Building and checking**: `make build`, `make check` (format, vet for this platform and Linux, race-enabled tests, lint, `govulncheck`), `make e2e` - the kernel tests as root, then the built binary in a lab of network namespaces: a bootstrap, joins and approvals, a crashed leader, a planned stop, a partition, removal, refusal, a wrong secret, a full stop, and three backends serving a service through IPVS - traffic spread, a failing application drained and back, the leader crashing with the services, a backend stopping, one removed - with nothing sent on the machine's own network; `make lab` builds the same lab to try by hand, `test/lab.sh` drives it - and `make remote-check` and `make remote-e2e` for a Linux machine over ssh.
- **`thelb backend`**: enrols on the control VIP with the backends' secret and waits for a director's approval (`admin approve`, as directors do); offers what it serves (`--offer tcp/8080,check=http:/healthz`, any number); sends its heartbeat every 2 seconds; holds the VIPs of the services its offers are assigned to on its own dummy interface, `thelb0`, with `arp_ignore` 1 and `arp_announce` 2, mapping ports where a service's differs from its application's; checks its applications; renews its certificate. On `SIGTERM` it tells the leader first, which drains it, then removes `thelb0` and restores its parameters. Removed by a director, it stops.
- **Pools, services and assignments**: `admin pool add <name> --vip <first/prefix> --vippool-count <n>` (inside its network, no network or broadcast address, no overlap with another pool, the control VIP or a node's address, on an interface the leader has there); `admin service add <name> --pool <pool> --proto tcp|udp|sctp --port <port>` takes the pool's lowest free address, probed on the network first; `admin assign <service> <node>:<proto>/<port> ... [--weight node=n]` checks that the offer is the backend's, its protocol the service's, and that the leader reaches the backend directly.
- **Port mapping**: an offer's port may differ from its service's - nginx on 8080 on one backend and on 9090 on another, the service on 80. The backend maps the port with one destination NAT rule per VIP, protocol and port, in theLB's own nftables table `inet thelb`, written over netlink and replaced whole in one transaction; connection tracking turns the replies back. The table exists only while a mapping does; a crash's leftover table is removed at the next start.
- **The data plane**: the leader adds the service VIPs to their pools' interfaces and announces them, programs IPVS over netlink - no `ipvsadm` - with direct routing and `wrr` by default, pins each backend's address to its MAC address, and sets `sloppy_tcp`, `expire_nodest_conn` and `expire_quiescent_template`, restoring them when it stops leading. A backend gets its weight only while both its own check and the leader's pass, its heartbeat is current and it holds the VIP; otherwise weight 0. A director that crashed removes the VIPs, IPVS services and pins it left when it starts again.
- **A director's host can be a backend too**: start `thelb backend` there after the director, under a name of its own - two daemons, each with its own socket (`/run/thelb/director/`, `/run/thelb/backend/`) and its own systemd unit. While that director leads, IPVS hands its own share to the local stack; while another leads, it is sent its share like any backend. `thelb status --role backend` shows the backend's own view.
- **IP-in-IP tunnelling**: `admin service add ... --method tunnel` reaches backends in other networks - the leader programs IPVS's IPIP forwarding; the backend unwraps on theLB's own interface `thelbt0`, relaxes reverse-path filtering only as far as it must (recorded and restored), and clamps its handshakes' TCP MSS so wrapped packets fit. A backend may join a control VIP it reaches through a router, and then serves tunnelled services only. IPv4 in this build.
- **The control VIP may be a name**: `--vip thelb-vip`, `--join thelb-vip:7443`, resolved at start to the one address it has in the node's networks.
- **Fixed**: an API stream's first deadline covered writes for its whole life, so the leader's writes failed 10 seconds into every backend's heartbeat connection - corrupting the TLS stream - and every director's check-in stream; a removed backend then retried a refused handshake in silence. Streams set a deadline per message; a backend logs each distinct failure, and enrols again when the cluster keeps refusing its certificate.
- **Dependencies**: hashicorp/raft and what it brings (go-hclog, go-metrics, go-msgpack, golang-lru, go-immutable-radix, and their own); everything else is Go's standard library.
